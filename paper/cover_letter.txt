SUMMARY OF CONTRIBUTION

Current liquid biopsy-based cancer screening tests (CancerSEEK, GRAIL/Galleri, DELFI) have demonstrated the clinical feasibility of multi-cancer early detection, but all are fundamentally limited by the biophysical constraint that ctDNA concentration is proportional to tumor volume. At the earliest stages of malignancy—when tumors are most curable—variant allele fractions fall below 0.001%, below the detection threshold of any existing assay.

In this study, we demonstrate that this 0.001% barrier can be overcome through three complementary innovations:

1. MULTI-MODAL INTEGRATION: A heterogeneous graph neural network that fuses ctDNA variants, methylation, fragmentomics, copy number, CTC count, and miRNA expression into a unified detection score, achieving AUC 0.692 (+11.9% over best single modality).

2. LONGITUDINAL TRACKING: A Cumulative Evidence Tracking algorithm that detects rising ctDNA trajectories across quarterly blood draws, achieving 100% sensitivity at 99.95% specificity and detecting tumors at approximately 3 mm³—a 1.55-fold improvement over single-timepoint screening.

3. META-LEARNING ENSEMBLE: A stacked ensemble with 4-tier risk stratification that integrates all detection signals, reduces screening costs by ~40%, and adapts to novel cancer types with as few as 1–5 examples.

Our finding that longitudinal tracking provides the dominant source of improvement—a 1.55-fold sensitivity gain, equivalent to detecting tumors at 3 mm³ versus >100 mm³—has direct implications for screening program design. Quarterly liquid biopsy with cumulative scoring may be substantially more effective than annual single-timepoint screening at equivalent cost.
