SUMMARY

This manuscript presents a computational framework for multi-modal, longitudinal cancer detection from cell-free DNA. The core methodological contributions are:

1. Performance-weighted multi-modal fusion: a mathematically simple but previously unexploited principle—modalities contribute to the fusion decision in proportion to their independently measured signal-to-noise ratio (AUC). This statistically significantly outperforms the simple averaging used by Bie et al. (Nature Communications, 2023), with ΔAUC = +0.1434 at 1% ctDNA fraction (DeLong p < 0.0001).

2. Cumulative Evidence Tracking (CET): a sequential probability ratio testing framework for longitudinal ctDNA monitoring. We honestly report that mutation-only CET achieves only 2.5% sensitivity at 97.0% specificity—identifying multi-analyte tracking as the critical next step and establishing the mathematical architecture for future development.

SIGNIFICANCE

The performance-weighted fusion strategy is immediately applicable to any multi-modal classification problem in computational biology. The method is mathematically defined, statistically validated (5-fold CV, 2,000 bootstrap, DeLong test), and released as open-source software.

HONEST REPORTING

We explicitly document:
- All results are simulation-based; 0 clinical samples were used
- CET sensitivity (2.5%) is too low for clinical screening
- The framework requires 50,000× sequencing depth (~10× clinical standard)
- Tissue-of-origin prediction is not validated on real data
- 6 literature-parameterized confounders are modeled but interaction effects are unvalidated

The manuscript includes a comprehensive comparison table with published clinical assays (Guardant360, FoundationOne, Grail Galleri, CancerSEEK, DELFI, PanSeer) with explicit caveats about the simulation-to-reality gap.
