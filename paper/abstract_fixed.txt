DeepCatch: A Multi-Modal Longitudinal Framework for Ultra-Early Cancer Detection — A Proof-of-Concept Simulation Study

CORRECTED ABSTRACT
(Post-review revision, April 28, 2026)

Liquid biopsy-based cancer screening faces a fundamental biophysical constraint: at the earliest disease stages, circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA) concentrations fall below 0.001% variant allele fraction (VAF), where Poisson sampling noise renders single-timepoint detection unreliable. We present DeepCatch, a computational proof-of-concept framework that integrates multi-modal ctDNA analysis with longitudinal trajectory tracking to explore the theoretical limits of ultra-early detection. This is a simulation study only; no real patient data were used.

DeepCatch comprises four components evaluated on synthetic cohorts designed to approximate ctDNA dynamics at 0.001% VAF: (1) a variant calling approach that detected 17% (5/30; 95% CI: 6–35%) of simulated variants at 0.001% VAF in a single run; (2) a heterogeneous graph neural network (GNN) for multi-modal fusion achieving AUC 0.692 (95% CI: 0.58–0.80, n=90 test patients), representing an 11.9% increase over the best single modality—a difference that is not statistically significant at this sample size; (3) a Cumulative Evidence Tracking (CET) algorithm that achieved 1.000 sensitivity (95% CI: 0.996–1.000) at 0.9995 specificity in a single simulation run, with detection corresponding under model assumptions to estimated tumor volumes of ~3 mm³—though this result has not been cross-validated; and (4) a stacked ensemble with 4-tier risk stratification that may reduce per-cancer-found costs by an estimated ~40% under simplified cost model assumptions.

Our findings suggest that combined multi-modal + longitudinal approaches may theoretically overcome single-timepoint Poisson noise floors at ultra-low ctDNA fractions. However, all results are derived from idealized simulations with a single random seed. Critical limitations include: no real patient validation, no cross-validation of the headline CET result, statistically underpowered fusion experiments, unmodeled clonal hematopoiesis (affecting 10–15% of screening-age individuals), idealized tumor growth and shedding assumptions, and economically prohibitive sequencing depth requirements (~50,000×). Model-based projections suggest that longitudinal tracking could provide detection advantages over single-timepoint approaches, but actual lead times, sensitivity, and specificity require prospective clinical validation. This study provides a computational framework for investigating the theoretical limits of ctDNA-based screening; clinical deployment depends on rigorous validation in real patient cohorts.
