We are pleased to submit our manuscript entitled "DeepCatch: Performance-Weighted Multi-Modal Fusion with Cumulative Evidence Tracking for Pan-Cancer Detection from Cell-Free DNA" for consideration as an Original Article in the Computational Methods track of Bioinformatics.

WHY BIOINFORMATICS?

DeepCatch is a computational methods paper. Our primary contributions are algorithmic and statistical: (1) a performance-weighted multi-modal fusion strategy that statistically significantly outperforms simple averaging (ΔAUC = +0.104, p < 0.0001, DeLong test); (2) the first application of sequential probability ratio testing (SPRT) to multi-timepoint, multi-analyte cancer screening; and (3) what we believe is the first tissue-of-origin classifier demonstrated at 20-cancer-type scale from simulated methylation and fragmentomic features. We believe these contributions align with Bioinformatics' focus on novel computational methods for biological data analysis.

WHAT IS NEW?

- Performance-weighted fusion: Unlike Bie et al. (2023, Nature Communications) which uses simple score averaging, DeepCatch weights each of five modalities (mutations, methylation, fragmentomics, copy number, CTC count) by its independently measured AUC. This is a simple but previously unexploited principle that yields statistically significant improvement.

- Multi-modal CET: Prior longitudinal cfDNA work (PanSeer, Chen et al. 2020) used single archived timepoints. DeepCatch models multi-timepoint trajectories with Gompertz growth, accumulating evidence across modalities via SPRT—demonstrating proof-of-concept even though clinical-grade sensitivity/specificity are not yet achieved.

- Pan-cancer scale with honest confounders: 20 cancer types (up from 8 in prior drafts), 6 literature-parameterized biological confounders (CHIP, variable shedding, trinucleotide errors, etc.), and explicit correction of an overfit methylation entropy claim (AUC 1.000 → 0.786).

- Cost-effective deployment: 5,000× targeted capture at $74/sample—matching clinical standard depth at research reagent cost.

HONESTY ABOUT LIMITATIONS

We want to be explicit: this is a simulation study. All results are derived from synthetic cohorts parameterized with real genomic frequencies from COSMIC v99 and TCGA PanCancer Atlas. No real patient plasma samples were used. We do not claim clinical utility—we claim a computational advance that requires prospective clinical validation. We believe honest simulation studies serve an important role in computational biology by exploring parameter spaces, developing methods, and identifying limitations before resources are committed to wet-lab work. Every table includes explicit caveats about the simulation-to-reality gap.
