DeepCatch: Performance-Weighted Multi-Modal Fusion with Cumulative Evidence Tracking for Ultra-Early Cancer Detection from Cell-Free DNA

Royce [Surname] and the DeepCatch Contributors

ABSTRACT

Motivation: Liquid biopsy-based cancer screening is fundamentally limited at ultra-low circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA) fractions (<0.1%), where Poisson sampling noise renders single-timepoint, single-modality detection unreliable. Multi-modal fusion and longitudinal evidence accumulation are theoretically complementary strategies, but their combined potential has not been systematically evaluated under realistic biological confounders.

Results: We present DeepCatch, a computational framework integrating performance-weighted multi-modal fusion with Cumulative Evidence Tracking (CET) for cancer detection from cell-free DNA. Using real mutation frequencies from COSMIC v99 and TCGA PanCancer Atlas across 8 cancer types, parameterized with 6 literature-derived confounders, DeepCatch's performance-weighted fusion achieves AUC 0.9610 at 1% ctDNA fraction—ΔAUC = +0.1434 over Bie et al. (2023) at matched ctDNA fraction (DeLong p < 0.0001). At matched modality count, DeepCatch achieves AUC 0.9784 (95% CI: 0.9721–0.9837) vs. Bie's 0.9668 (ΔAUC = +0.0116, p < 0.0001). The CET longitudinal module achieves 97.0% specificity but only 2.5% sensitivity with mutation-only tracking—identifying a critical limitation of single-analyte longitudinal monitoring.

Availability: All code, synthetic data generators, and reproduction scripts are available at https://github.com/deepcatch-consortium/deepcatch under the MIT license. Results are fully reproducible via RUN_ALL.sh. No real patient data were used; all results are simulation-based.
